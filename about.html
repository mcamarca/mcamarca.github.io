About Me

My planetary astronomy story begins my senior year of college at Marymount University in Arlington, VA. I was soon to graduate with my degree in biology, and came across an article in National Geographic about the moons of Jupiter. From reading that article, I learned for the first time that there are ocean-bearing moons in our Solar System, and that these distant worlds are incredibly geologically active. Learning about the richness of surface processes available on the Galilean moons was so exciting to me that I decided that planetary astronomy had to become my new intellectual home. I graduated with my biology degree from Marymount in May 2017, and just a few weeks later moved to Greenbelt, MD to begin a summer internship on infrared spectroscopy of comets at NASA Goddard Space Flight Center. This internship turned into a postbac for me, and during this time I took classes part-time in math and physics at American University in Washington, D.C. to prepare for graduate school. In Fall 2019, I moved to Pasadena to begin my PhD studies at Caltech in the planetary science department with Prof. Katherine de Kleer as my thesis advisor. I received my doctorate in June 2025, and am currently a postdoc at Caltech working with Profs. Jonathan Lunine and Katherine de Kleer.
